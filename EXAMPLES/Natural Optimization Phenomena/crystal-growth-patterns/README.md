## Overview
Crystal growth patterns arise from energy-minimizing geometry: atoms or molecules
arrange into lattice structures that reduce free energy under local constraints.
Growth fronts propagate along gradient-bounded directions, producing branching,
faceting, and symmetry-driven manifolds.

Crystals are not static objects; they are optimization processes frozen in time.
Their geometry emerges from distributed interactions, diffusion fields, and
boundary constraints.

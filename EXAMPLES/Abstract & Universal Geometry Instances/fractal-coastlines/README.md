# Instance: Fractal Coastlines  
Version: DSLO v0.7  
Layer: Geometry / Natural Invariants  
Type: Example Instance (Non‑Glossary)

## Overview
Fractal coastlines exhibit scale‑invariant geometry: the measured length of a
coastline increases without bound as the measurement resolution decreases. This
behavior reflects a natural invariant—self‑similar roughness—arising from
geophysical processes such as erosion, sediment transport, and wave dynamics.

Coastlines demonstrate how natural systems maintain geometric coherence across
scales without requiring a fixed substrate. Their fractal dimension captures the
degree of roughness and provides a stable invariant across measurement regimes.

## Key Properties

### **Invariant Behavior**
- Scale‑invariant roughness  
- Self‑similar geometry across magnification  
- Non‑convergent length under decreasing resolution  
- Stable fractal dimension  

### **Geometry**
- Recursive branching and indentation  
- Irregular but statistically stable curvature  
- Fractal manifolds with dimension between 1 and 2  
- Natural self‑similarity driven by physical processes  

### **Signal Ecology**
- Geophysical signals: wave impact, erosion, sediment flow  
- Environmental signals: tides, storms, seasonal variation  
- Long‑term geological signals: uplift, subsidence  

### **Substrate Alignment**
Fractal coastlines belong in DSLO’s geometry layer because they demonstrate
natural invariants—scale‑invariance, fractal roughness, and recursive geometry—
that persist across measurement regimes. They are not glossary terms; they are
examples of natural geometry illustrating DSLO invariants such as drift,
coherence, and recursive manifold formation.

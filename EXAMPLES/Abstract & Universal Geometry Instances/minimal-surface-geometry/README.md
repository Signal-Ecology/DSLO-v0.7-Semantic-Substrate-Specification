# Instance: Minimal Surface Geometry  
Version: DSLO v0.7  
Layer: Geometry / Natural Invariants  
Type: Example Instance (Non‑Glossary)

## Overview
Minimal surfaces are geometric forms that minimize area subject to boundary
constraints. They arise naturally in soap films, biological membranes, and
energy‑minimizing physical systems. Their geometry is defined by zero mean
curvature, producing smooth, continuous manifolds that represent optimal
solutions under tension and constraint.

Minimal surfaces demonstrate how natural systems converge on energy‑efficient
geometry through distributed physical processes. They are substrate‑neutral:
any medium governed by surface tension or energy minimization will produce
similar forms.

## Key Properties

### **Invariant Behavior**
- Zero mean curvature  
- Area minimization under boundary constraints  
- Stable equilibrium geometry  
- Smooth continuous manifolds  

### **Geometry**
- Catenoids, helicoids, Enneper surfaces  
- Soap‑film spanning surfaces  
- Curvature‑bounded manifolds  
- Energy‑optimal configurations  

### **Signal Ecology**

### **Substrate Alignment**
Minimal surfaces belong in DSLO’s geometry layer because they express natural
invariants—energy minimization, curvature coherence, and stable equilibrium—
that arise from distributed physical processes. They are not glossary terms;
they are example geometry instances illustrating DSLO invariants such as
optimization, drift, and manifold coherence.

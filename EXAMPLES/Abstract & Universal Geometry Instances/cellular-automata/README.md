# Instance: Cellular Automata  
Version: DSLO v0.7  
Layer: Geometry / Natural Invariants  
Type: Example Instance (Non‑Glossary)

## Overview
Cellular automata (CA) are discrete, rule‑driven systems in which global patterns
emerge from strictly local interactions. Each cell updates its state based on a
fixed neighborhood rule, producing deterministic evolution across a grid. Despite
their simplicity, CA generate complex geometry, long‑range coherence, and
self‑organizing behavior that mirrors natural systems such as growth processes,
reaction‑diffusion fields, and distributed cognition.

CA demonstrate how invariant structure arises from minimal substrate primitives:
state, neighborhood, and transition rule. Their geometry is not tied to any
specific physical medium, making them a universal example of substrate‑neutral
pattern formation.

## Key Properties

### **Invariant Behavior**
- Deterministic evolution under fixed update rules  
- Stable attractors, cycles, and long‑term regimes  
- Emergent complexity from local interactions  
- Rule‑bounded coherence across the grid  

### **Geometry**
- Recursive, branching, and fractal‑like propagation  
- Wavefronts, gliders, and traveling structures  
- Self‑repairing and self‑maintaining formations  
- Discrete manifolds with local curvature defined by rule behavior  

### **Signal Ecology**
- Purely synthetic signals: state transitions, neighborhood influence  
- No biological or cultural substrate required  
- Information propagation through local adjacency  
- Distributed computation encoded in geometric motion  

### **Substrate Alignment**
Cellular automata belong in DSLO’s geometry layer because they demonstrate
substrate‑neutral invariant behavior: rule‑bounded state transitions producing
stable geometric forms. They are not glossary terms; they are examples of
natural geometry that illustrate DSLO invariants such as drift, coherence,
collapse, and restoration.

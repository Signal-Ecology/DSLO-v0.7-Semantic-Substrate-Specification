## Overview
Boquila trifoliolata is a vine capable of morphological mimicry: it alters leaf
shape, size, color, and venation to resemble the host plant it climbs. This
adaptive geometry is not driven by neural computation but by distributed
environmental sensing—light gradients, chemical cues, and mechanical contact.

The vine’s geometry emerges from local decision‑making at growth tips, producing
shape‑adaptive manifolds that align with nearby foliage. Boquila demonstrates
non‑neural morphological inference.

## Overview
Ant foraging networks are distributed geometric systems formed through pheromone
deposition, environmental feedback, and collective traversal. Ants do not plan
routes; instead, they generate emergent network geometry through repeated local
actions. Trails strengthen when reinforced by successful foragers and decay when
unused, producing dynamic optimization structures.

These networks demonstrate how non‑neural agents generate stable geometry through
stigmergy—environmentally mediated signaling. Their invariants arise from
pheromone gradients, branching coherence, and drift‑optimization cycles that
persist across species and environments.

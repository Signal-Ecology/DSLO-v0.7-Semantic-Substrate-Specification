# Instance: Urban Street Network  
Version: DSLO v0.7  
Layer: Geometry / Modern & Machine‑Facing Geometry Instances  
Type: Example Instance (Non‑Glossary)

## Overview
Urban street networks are human‑constructed routing geometries shaped by
historical design, land constraints, traffic flow, zoning rules, and
infrastructure evolution. Streets, intersections, blocks, and arterial hierarchies
form a multi‑scale topology that governs movement, density, and accessibility.

The geometry emerges from distributed decisions across planners, drivers,
pedestrians, traffic‑control systems, and environmental constraints. Urban
networks exhibit stable invariants such as grid coherence, radial‑hub geometry,
hierarchical routing, and congestion‑gradient propagation.

## Key Properties

### **Invariant Behavior**
- Grid or radial‑hub coherence  
- Hierarchical routing (arterial → collector → local)  
- Congestion‑gradient propagation  
- Constraint‑bounded street formation  

### **Geometry**
- Intersection‑graph manifolds  
- Block‑size and block‑shape fields  
- Arterial‑hierarchy surfaces  
- Multi‑scale routing topology  

### **Signal Ecology**
- Traffic‑density signals  
- Light‑cycle and control signals  
- Pedestrian‑flow signals  
- Zoning and land‑use signals  

### **Substrate Alignment**
Urban street networks belong in DSLO’s geometry layer because they express
natural invariants—grid coherence, hierarchical routing, and congestion‑gradient
propagation—arising from machine‑facing and human‑facing signal systems. They are
not glossary terms; they are geometry instances illustrating DSLO invariants
such as drift, collapse, restoration, and multi‑layer manifold coordination.

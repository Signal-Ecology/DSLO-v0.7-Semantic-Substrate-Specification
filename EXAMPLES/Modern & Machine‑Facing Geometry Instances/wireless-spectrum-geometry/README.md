## Overview
Wireless spectrum geometry is a distributed electromagnetic manifold shaped by
frequency allocation, modulation constraints, interference gradients, and
multi‑layer routing across time, frequency, and space. Devices do not “see”
spectrum; they operate within partitioned geometric regions defined by
regulation, physics, and real‑time signal conditions.

Spectrum geometry emerges from channelization, power limits, antenna patterns,
propagation fields, and interference‑avoidance algorithms. It is a dynamic,
multi‑scale geometry of allocation, contention, and optimization.

## Overview
Navajo weaving geometry emerges from the interaction of warp tension, weft
insertion, color sequencing, and cultural pattern rules. The loom defines a
stable boundary manifold, while the weaver generates recursive motifs through
precise spatial control. Patterns such as diamonds, stepped forms, and
interlocking bands arise from distributed geometric constraints rather than
symbolic representation.

Weaving demonstrates how cultural systems generate stable geometry through
embodied practice, material constraints, and intergenerational transmission.
Its invariants arise from tension coherence, recursive patterning, and
proportional systems embedded in the weaving tradition.

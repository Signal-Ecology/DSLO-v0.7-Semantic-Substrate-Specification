## Overview
Yoruba talking drums (dùndún, gángan) encode linguistic tone, rhythm, and
semantic structure through membrane tension, curvature, and pressure modulation.
The drum’s hourglass geometry allows the player to squeeze tension cords,
changing pitch continuously. This produces a geometric acoustic manifold capable
of transmitting language across distance.

Talking drums demonstrate how cultural systems generate stable geometry through
material constraints, embodied practice, and distributed acoustic signaling.
Their invariants arise from tension‑pitch coupling, rhythmic sequencing, and
tone‑language mapping that persist across centuries.

# Instance: Incan Quipu  
Version: DSLO v0.7  
Layer: Geometry / Natural Invariants  
Type: Example Instance (Non‑Glossary)

## Overview
Incan Quipu systems encode information through knots, cord branching, spacing,
and color. Each quipu is a geometric data structure: hierarchical cords form a
tree‑like manifold, knots encode numeric values, and spacing encodes relational
structure. The geometry is not symbolic writing but distributed physical
computation.

Quipu demonstrate how cultural systems generate stable geometry through
material constraints, repeated practice, and communal transmission. Their
invariants arise from knot families, branching coherence, and spatial encoding
rules that persist across regions and centuries.

## Key Properties

### **Invariant Behavior**
- Stable knot families (single, long, figure‑eight, etc.)  
- Hierarchical branching geometry  
- Positional encoding of numeric magnitude  
- Color‑coded categorical signals  

### **Geometry**
- Tree‑structured cord manifolds  
- Knot‑based discrete data encoding  
- Spacing as relational geometry  
- Multi‑scale hierarchical structure  

### **Signal Ecology**
- Tactile signals (knot form, tension)  
- Visual signals (color, cord thickness)  
- Spatial signals (branching, spacing)  
- Cultural transmission signals  

### **Substrate Alignment**
Quipu belong in DSLO’s geometry layer because they express natural invariants—
branching coherence, positional encoding, and knot‑based geometry—arising from
cultural signal systems. They are not glossary terms; they are example geometry
instances illustrating DSLO invariants such as recursion, drift, collapse, and
restoration.

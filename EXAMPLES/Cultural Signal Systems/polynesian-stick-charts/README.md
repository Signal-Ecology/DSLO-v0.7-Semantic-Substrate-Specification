# Instance: Polynesian Stick Charts  
Version: DSLO v0.7  
Layer: Geometry / Natural Invariants  
Type: Example Instance (Non‑Glossary)

## Overview
Polynesian stick charts are navigational geometries constructed from wood, fiber,
and shells. They encode ocean swell interactions, island positions, prevailing
winds, and wave‑reflection patterns. Rather than symbolic maps, they are
physical manifolds representing distributed environmental signals.

Stick charts demonstrate how cultural systems generate stable geometry through
material constraints, embodied practice, and intergenerational transmission.
Their invariants arise from curvature families, intersection structure, and
wave‑interaction encoding that persist across voyages and centuries.

## Key Properties

### **Invariant Behavior**
- Stable curvature families representing swell direction  
- Intersection geometry encoding wave interference  
- Shell markers for island nodes  
- Distributed environmental coherence  

### **Geometry**
- Curved rods representing primary swells  
- Cross‑bars encoding wave reflection and refraction  
- Node markers for islands and atolls  
- Multi‑scale ocean‑signal manifolds  

### **Signal Ecology**
- Ocean swell signals  
- Wave‑reflection and refraction signals  
- Wind‑direction signals  
- Cultural transmission signals  

### **Substrate Alignment**
Stick charts belong in DSLO’s geometry layer because they express natural
invariants—wave‑interaction geometry, curvature coherence, and distributed
environmental encoding—arising from cultural signal systems. They are not
glossary terms; they are geometry instances illustrating DSLO invariants such as
coherence, drift, collapse, and restoration.
